**Verdict: build it as its own overlay on the radar's shell, fed by one PHP-cached national grid from MET Nordic. Not a chip in the measured-now map, not Open-Meteo, not the browser-to-thredds path.**

**1. Where it lives.** A separate map opened like the radar: make the hero wind value "↘ 6 (10) m/s" tappable and it opens a full map with a timeline — reusing the overlay shell "Vis nedbørradar" already has (open/close, play, scrub strip, attribution). One door, zero permanent page furniture, which is the actual constraint. I'd refuse the chip in the local map: that map's promise is *målt nå* — Netatmo numbers per cell, a caption that carefully separates measured from forecast ("varslet nå 12,7° · ingen stasjoner"). Forecast particles want the basemap to themselves (dimmed labels, no number labels) and a national-to-regional view; in the hero's ~400 px box 900 particles are confetti and 20 km cells are mush. Two kinds of wind in one map ("Vind nå" measured vs a forecast field) breaks the measured/forecast contract the page keeps elsewhere. A second door (a "Vindkart" link in the Vind tab) can come later if logs say people miss the number; the shared Kart/Radar/Vind/Skygge switcher is the right v2 consolidation of three working doors, not before the feature proves itself. Phone: same door (the value sits near the top of the now card), full-screen overlay, timeline docked at the bottom, ~44 px play button.

**2. Data path.** MET Nordic 1 km through our PHP, repacked and cached — `api/windgrid.php`. Not browser→thredds: CORS `*` is nice, but every visitor eats the 5.3 s time-dimension slowness and ~1.7 MB of XDR, you ship a .dods parser, and a research OPeNDAP endpoint becomes load-bearing — the class of flaky API that just taught Kjørevær to back off Geonorge. Not Open-Meteo, not the mix: a 20×20 grid for 2 days is 80 % of OMGate's minute budget and competes with the 13-model forecast that is the site's reason to exist; even 12×12 is ~30 %. Disqualified. Not Locationforecast-per-point: 400 PHP calls per cached grid is intelmap's shape, and rude. Concretely: on first request after a new model issue, PHP fetches the NetCDF once (`flock` against stampedes, hard 20 s timeout), strides the Lambert grid to ~20 km (≈90×117 over Norway + margin), steps hourly to +24 h then 3-hourly to +54 h (35 steps), computes u/v server-side, and packs **one byte per component (0.4 m/s steps, 128 = null — carries nulls, never zero-fills, so sea and edges stay honest)**. 10 530 × 35 × 2 ≈ 700 KB raw, ~300 KB gzipped, one fetch per map open, no Open-Meteo weight. File cache keyed on the issue, serve stale up to 6 h with the issue time shown. Extent fixed, not viewport-adaptive — intelmap's refetch-on-pan makes every pan a new cache key and a cold 5 s path on hosting with no trusted cron. Two additions: resample to Web Mercator in the same PHP pass (the file ships per-point lat/lon arrays, so it's a one-time bilinear remap) — a Lambert grid dropped on a Mercator map as a plain image overlay sits warped by a couple of kilometres, and "the arrow is over the wrong valley" is the bug report you don't want. And skip the dense zoom-in box for v1; cap the map around z10 where the grid is still honest and say "grovt rutenett" in the caption.

**3. Timeline.** 35 steps, scrubber on the data steps, plus a play button (~0.6 s/step, loop) — intelmap's no-play timeline is a diagram, and the radar shell already has the player. **No inter-step field interpolation in v1**: hourly steps change direction by a few degrees; crossfade the colour field ~300 ms on step change and let particles read the nearest step — full u/v lerp means two frame sources disagreeing mid-play for a difference nobody can see. Opens at the hour table's selected hour, else now; it keeps its own hour like the snow layer's slider (`lm.snowIdx`), one-way, no write-back — two-way silently changes the table under the user. Labels in the table's idiom ("i dag 14:00"), ticks every 6 h.

**4. Rendering.** Hand-rolled on Leaflet, two layers — not leaflet-velocity (unmaintained, drags its own field model), not WebGL (webgl-wind is a static global demo; this site's superpower is no build step). But don't copy intelmap's per-pan CPU repaint: bake the field once per step into a grid-sized offscreen canvas (~90×140 px) through a 256-entry LUT and add it as a Leaflet ImageOverlay anchored at the grid corner — Leaflet transforms it for free, pans stay smooth, the same LUT drives the legend, and the pre-baked canvases make the crossfade free. Particles on a canvas above: ~900 desktop, ~450 phone (scale with area), DPR capped at 2, destination-out trails, screen speed ∝ √(m/s) capped where a grid cell is ~40 px so zooming in stops the crawl before it lies. `visibilitychange` pauses; closing kills RAF and any fetch. `prefers-reduced-motion`: static arrow field on the colour field — the fallback must still be a wind map, not a notice. Palette: **grey-blue → teal → indigo → violet, Beaufort-banded.** Not the yr green ramp: on this page green already means rain (radar, Regn) and mild temperature — a wind ramp through green will be misread, and violet at the top means a storm never looks like heavy rain. Legend = the temperature map's gradient bar, m/s ticks with the Norwegian Beaufort names ("frisk bris", "liten kuling"…), which need no key.

**5. Cut list, and the risk.** Cut for v1: gust colouring (third variable = +50 % of every byte; the "6 (10)" toggle is v2's first line), WebGL, viewport/zoom-adaptive resolution, Open-Meteo anything, two-way hour sync, the Kart unification, the dense zoom box. Keep the tap-probe — ten lines on data already client-side, and checking a mountain pass is why someone opens this map. Outside the Nordic file: dim the door with "Vindkart dekker Norden" rather than building a mixed data path. Biggest risk: **thredds failing exactly when the map matters most** — storms drive peak traffic and are when OPeNDAP 503s, on hosting with no reliable cron. The per-issue cache makes Glett ~a dozen fetches a day; serve-stale + timestamp keeps it alive; the timeout stops a cold fetch blowing PHP's execution limit; and when all that fails, fail quiet — hide the door the way the local map already says "ingen stasjoner", never a spinner that hangs. Second risk: 20 km smoothing invents calm in fjords and mountain gaps where the real wind is channelled and nasty — the caption must confess "prognose, grovt rutenett" the way the temperature map's caption confesses its cells and station mix.
